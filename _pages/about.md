## About Me

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Konstanz, specializing in stress research and psychophysiology. My work focuses on understanding physiological stress responses and their regulation through environmental and technological interventions.

I completed my doctoral degree at the University of Konstanz in 2025, where I investigated the relaxing effects of virtual environments on the autonomic nervous system. My dissertation examined heart rate variability as a key indicator of physiological stress regulation in virtual reality contexts.

**Research Interests:**
- Psychophysiological stress regulation
- Virtual reality and mental health
- Heart rate variability and autonomic nervous system function
- Environmental psychology
- Human-computer interaction in therapeutic contexts

My current research continues to explore the intersection of technology, environment, and human physiology, with implications for stress management and well-being interventions.

Bridging Theory and Practice
======
I am driven by the synergy between applied psychotherapy and data-driven research. My goal is to translate complex psychophysiological findings into meaningful clinical interventions, ensuring that therapeutic practices are grounded in rigorous empirical evidence. To maintain the highest standards of Open Science, I perform my data analysis primarily in R, leveraging reproducible workflows to uncover the intricate patterns of human stress and regulation. I believe that the future of mental health lies at the intersection of clinical expertise and sophisticated statistical modeling.
